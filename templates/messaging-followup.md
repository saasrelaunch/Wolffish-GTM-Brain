# Template, messaging app follow-up

Audience: one named person who has already been introduced. Channel: messaging apps, an accelerant
across the other channels and never a first touch.

## The precondition
A message to a Saudi executive who has not been introduced is not a channel, it is an intrusion.
If no introduction has landed, stop and use `warm-introduction.md` instead. This precondition is a
rule, not a courtesy.

## Structure
- **One line of context.** Where you met or who introduced you, so the reader places you without
  scrolling.
- **One substantive thing.** A regulatory update, a number, a document. Something they would have
  wanted even if they never buy.
- **One ask, or none.** A message with two asks gets neither.
- **Nothing attached on the first message.** A document arrives after the reply, not before it.

## Length
Under sixty words. Written as one person writing to another, not as a company writing to a
prospect. Arabic if the relationship is conducted in Arabic, and then fully in Arabic rather than
mixed.

## Cadence
One message. If there is no reply, wait a full week before a second and never send a third. The
follow-up that damages the relationship damages the sponsor who made the introduction, which costs
far more than the deal.

## Fields to fill
- Recipient and how you were introduced: [name, sponsor]
- The substantive thing: [what they get from this message even if they never buy]
- The ask, if any: [one]

## What this channel may never carry
No price negotiation. No contractual term. No security answer that will be evaluated later. Those
belong in a document that can be filed and audited, and a security officer who receives one over a
messaging app learns something about how this vendor handles evidence.
