**The assumptions, so you can move them**

Seat count 300, twelve-month term. Usage assumption is a knowledge worker running agents through a
normal working day, which is what produces the SAR 45 to 55 band. The band moves with the model you
choose and with the quota tier you set, and it is your bill either way. We do not mark it up and we
do not see it, which is why we can be relaxed about you driving it down.

**What sits outside this**

Anything beyond the standard deployment rung: more than three custom integrations, or a scope that
requires work on systems we have not seen. Those are quoted separately against the scope ladder
before signature, never after.

**The comparison you asked for**

Microsoft's assistant is cheaper per seat than our landed cost and I would rather you hear that
from me. The argument for us is not price, it is that the inference runs on your account and the
audit record lands in your store. If the governance question is not live for you, the cheaper
product is the right answer and I will say so.

**The return anchor**

At 300 knowledge workers, this repays itself at a productivity gain of 0.77 percent, using a
modelled fully loaded cost of SAR 200,000 per worker. Replace that figure with your own during the
exposure audit and the number gets stronger.

## Why this works

**Three lines, always, with the pass-through named as customer-billed.** This is the single rule
this asset exists to enforce. Quoting SAR 79 against a competitor seat price hides a third of the
cost and a finance director finds it in the first meeting, at which point every other number in the
email is suspect.

**The assumptions are separated and movable.** A cost email that cannot be recomputed by the reader
is a claim. One that can be is a model, and models get forwarded to the people who decide.

**It concedes the price comparison first and voluntarily.** Saying plainly that the incumbent
assistant is cheaper is what buys the right to argue governance. Delivered the other way round it
reads as a vendor discovering an objection.

**The return anchor is arithmetic, not a promise.** 0.77 percent is small enough to be believed and
the modelled input is flagged as replaceable, which invites the buyer to improve the case
themselves.

**No discount appears.** Pricing is published and fixed, and a discount offered in writing before it
was asked for concedes the strongest position this company has in a market of quote-only
competitors.

**The phrase no new invoice does not appear anywhere.** The capability allowance is proposed and not
contracted. Until it is, that phrase is banned in writing.
