# ICP and buying committee

Distils the ICP dashboard and the buying committee dashboard. Load this before any targeting,
qualification or persona-specific writing.

## ICP 1, the founder-led motion

Saudi-owned, regulated or intellectual-property-sensitive enterprise. **200 to 600 knowledge
workers**, which is the seat count, not total headcount. Total headcount is often 500 to 3,000.

Sub-industries, in priority order: insurance and takaful, mid-tier banks and licensed finance
companies, private healthcare groups, law firms and professional services, engineering and
industrial groups, family holding companies with a shared services centre. Riyadh first, the
Eastern Province second because it is industrial and unusually receptive to on-device
architectures, Jeddah third.

**The strongest qualifying signal is an organisation that has already run an artificial
intelligence pilot that stalled.** They have spent the enthusiasm and now want the governance
parts, which is exactly what Wolffish sells.

Eleven attributes exist only in this market and they do most of the qualifying work: the
regulator, the data classification exposure, ownership concentration, the procurement route,
the workforce localisation band, the Arabic to English mix of knowledge workers, entity status,
budget cycle alignment, decision authority, the relationship sponsor path, and current posture
toward artificial intelligence.

Ownership concentration is a speed attribute. When the owner decides, procurement executes rather
than deliberates.

## ICP 2, the channel-assisted motion

Regulated large enterprises and government-adjacent entities. **600 to 2,500 knowledge workers**,
deployed in phases by business unit. Tier-one banks, large insurers, sovereign fund portfolio
companies, giga-project subsidiaries, semi-government authorities, large healthcare networks,
telecommunications and utilities.

Bought through a public tender platform, a framework call-off, or as a subcontract under an
integrator's prime agreement. Cycles run roughly double ICP 1.

## The qualification floor

**200 seats, and it is hard.** Year-one Wolffish revenue is SAR 60,000 plus SAR 948 per seat,
while deployment consumes six to eight weeks of principal engineering regardless of seat count.
Below 200 seats the engagement cannot repay that time at any point in the contract.

| Seats | Year 1 | Year 2 onward | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | SAR 154,800 | SAR 94,800 | Decline |
| 200 | SAR 249,600 | SAR 189,600 | Floor, only with a reference or expansion path |
| 300 | SAR 344,400 | SAR 284,400 | Target |
| 500 | SAR 534,000 | SAR 474,000 | Ideal, same principal cost, twice the annuity |

Two 500-seat customers are worth more than five 200-seat customers and cost less than half the
delivery capacity.

## The anti-ICP

Decline on the first call. A wrong customer does not cost a lost deal, it costs a quarter of the
year's delivery capacity.

Fewer than 200 knowledge workers. No named information security owner, because then the strongest
argument has no audience. No accessible decision authority. No internal or legacy system of
consequence, which removes the integration advantage and reduces the deal to a harness comparison
Wolffish does not win. A pure engineering organisation, where the developer coding tools are
better and cheaper and saying so builds more credibility than the deal is worth. Anyone who wants
a proof of concept before the security call, because that inverts the motion and spends principal
time before qualification.
