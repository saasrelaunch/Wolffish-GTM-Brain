# Competitor battlecards

Synthesized, not extracted. The competitive analysis holds scores; this holds the three lines an
operator says out loud. Each card leads with the rival's genuine strength, because that is what makes
it credible. Check this file before asserting anything about a competitor: a stale rumour repeated to
a Saudi buyer is an avoidable credibility loss.

## Microsoft, the bundled assistant

**Strength.** Ubiquity. It does not need to be chosen, only not rejected. Bundled into a renewal
that was happening anyway. A Saudi cloud region is announced for the fourth quarter of 2026.

**Gap.** It drafts inside documents rather than executing on the device with file and command
permissions. Its own reviewers name auditing as the weak point, and custom internal tooling.

**How we win.** Concede the price openly, it is cheaper per seat, then move to what each is allowed to do and who owns the audit log. Never fight on ecosystem breadth.

## OpenAI, the default brand

**Strength.** The brand is the noun for the category, so a proposal starts with the internal
argument half won. Serious administrative controls, broad connector catalogue.

**Gap.** No data residency in the Kingdom, quote-only pricing with a seat floor, and their own
guidance directs on-premises and air-gapped buyers to specialist vendors, which under Saudi cloud and
central-bank rules is a large share of the addressable base.

**How we win.** Quote their own guidance. It is the most useful sentence any competitor has written
on Wolffish's behalf.

## Anthropic, the capability leader

**Strength.** Frontier reasoning and the reference agentic coding tool. Very low seat price, and engineering affection that carries it into procurement from the bottom up.

**Gap.** Cost is unforecastable by design: zero bundled tokens, everything at usage rates. The
public 2026 record of budget exhaustion at large customers is the argument, and others made it.

**How we win.** Concede model quality without hesitation, then attack the invoice shape rather than
the product.

## The national champion

**Strength.** Sovereign legitimacy, national mandate, capital nobody matches, an Arabic model, and
in-Kingdom compute by definition. The answer nobody is criticised for choosing.

**Gap.** Shape rather than strength. Infrastructure, models and a marketplace, rather than a
deployed per-employee agent wired into one company's undocumented administration tool.

**How we win. We do not.** Neutralise in one sentence: different products, we run on their endpoint
if you want their model, you will probably buy both. Out-sovereigning the sovereign champion is the
fastest way to lose a Saudi deal.

## The Saudi systems integrator

**Strength.** They win before the evaluation starts, absorbed into an existing master agreement with
no vendor onboarding, no security review and no procurement event, on relationships that predate the
current technology lead.

**Gap.** Time-and-materials economics never end. Every capability is a new quote, there is no product
to inherit improvements from, and what lands is usually a chat interface over a search index.

**How we win.** Most deals are decided here. Product economics against project economics. Ask who
owns it in year three and what the fourth use case costs. Escrow answers the size objection.

## Developer coding tools

**Strength.** Real affection from engineers, fastest adoption path in the building, no committee.
**Gap.** Repository-scoped and developer-only. They touch no finance, legal or operations desk.

**How we win.** Do not displace them. A company already paying for one has proven agentic budget,
an engineering champion, and a finance director who has seen a metered invoice. Qualifying signal.

## The do-it-yourself open-source stack

**Strength.** Zero licence cost, complete sovereignty, and a capable platform team can demonstrate
something in a fortnight. What an ambitious head of digital proposes after Wolffish leaves.
**Gap.** No enforced quota, no attributed audit, no approval gates, no offboarding, no service level,
and two to four salaried engineers of maintenance that never reaches the business case.

**How we win.** Never dismiss it and never argue price, the modelled gap is small. Move to the
artefacts a regulator asks for, which the assembled stack does not produce.
