# Buying committee

Distils the buying committee dashboard. Load this before any persona-specific writing, and before
planning the order of meetings in an account.

## Seven roles in ICP 1

The three classic levels are the knowledge worker, the department head and the owner. Four more
decide Saudi deals, and a three-persona model has no slot for any of them.

## The classic three

**Knowledge worker.** Underwriter, claims analyst, credit analyst, associate counsel, design
engineer, financial analyst. Low formal influence, high veto by non-adoption: they cannot buy, and
they can silently kill a deployment by not using it, which is how most enterprise artificial
intelligence programmes in the Kingdom have died so far. Wins on getting home earlier and on working
in Arabic without being a second-class user of their own company's tooling.

**Department head.** Nominates the pilot cohort, defines the first workflow, and their verdict at
week three of the pilot is usually the verdict. Wins on output per head without a headcount request.
Blocks when the pilot threatens a delivery deadline, or when a previous pilot promised and did not
land.

**Owner or chief executive.** Decisive. In a concentrated-ownership Saudi company one conversation
here is worth six weeks of committee work. Wins on a cost line they control and on being able to
answer the board and the regulator in the same sentence. Blocks on "we already pay Microsoft", "why
not the national champion", and "two people is too small".

## The four the standard model omits

**The security officer, the gatekeeper.** Absolute veto, and frequently the real decision maker. In
a central-bank-regulated entity no cloud contract can be signed without prior regulator approval,
and that approval routes through this person. Wins on producing evidence an auditor accepts and on
being the security function that enabled rather than blocked. Blocks on shell access described
before approval gates are explained, on the absence of any certification, and on two-person
concentration risk. The asset that moves them is the Compliance Evidence Pack, and the security call
itself.

**The finance director, the economic validator.** Can defer indefinitely by declining to create a
budget line. Rarely champions, frequently kills. Wins on a number that turns out to be right. Blocks
on an inability to forecast a new category and on a saving that looks too large to be real.
Volunteer the pass-through line before they find it: a finance director who discovers a hidden third
of the cost will treat everything else in the pitch as similarly incomplete.

**Procurement and legal, the procedural gate.** Cannot approve a foreign-jurisdiction contract or a
non-compliant invoice without an exception process most will not initiate. Wins on a clean file: a
Saudi-jurisdiction contract in both languages, a compliant electronic invoice, a pre-drafted escrow
deed with a named agent. Never prospected. Prepare for them in advance rather than finding them,
which is why the evidence pack is built once and reused.

**The relationship sponsor, the introduction path.** No formal role, and they set the starting
position. A deal that arrives through a trusted introduction starts at trust and negotiates price. A
cold deal starts at suspicion and spends two quarters negotiating trust, if it survives. What they
need is a paragraph they can forward without endorsing, under eighty words, with no attachment and
no tracked link. A tracked link inside a personal introduction reads as a campaign and costs the
sponsor's credibility, which is the one thing that cannot be rebought.

## Three more in ICP 2

**Enterprise architect.** High blocking power, exercised before evaluation begins. Satisfied by an
architecture document mapped to their reference model showing that Wolffish adds no new external
dependency, which is unusual enough to state explicitly.

**Internal audit.** Medium blocking power, exercised late and expensively. Satisfied by the audit
log schema and a walkthrough of how a specific past action would be reconstructed.

**Programme management office.** High, and invisible until it defers you two quarters. Not hostile,
sequencing. The answer is portfolio entry before the budget cycle closes, not escalation.

## The sequence

Sponsor introduction. Then the security officer, with the decision maker present if possible. Then
the technology lead, for the integration and the "could we build this ourselves" conversation, held
deliberately rather than avoided. Then finance, then the pilot cohort. Then the decision maker,
given a pilot result, a convinced security officer and a finance director who has the number, rather
than asked to evaluate a vendor. Finally procurement, handed a complete pack, not a request.

**This is the highest-return process decision in the go-to-market.** The standard Western sequence
starts with the economic buyer and treats security as a late compliance hurdle, which here produces
a deal that dies in month four.
