# Pricing model

Distils the pricing and packaging dashboard. Load before any cost conversation, proposal or
competitor comparison. **All figures exclude the fifteen percent value-added tax**, which appears on
every quotation and every electronic invoice.

## Three lines, and only two are Wolffish revenue

| Line | Amount | Billed by | Wolffish margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deployment, one time | SAR 60,000 | Wolffish, half at signature and half at go-live | Yes |
| Platform seat | SAR 79 per employee per month | Wolffish, quarterly in advance, twelve-month term | Yes |
| Inference and infrastructure | SAR 45 to 55 per seat per month | **The customer's own providers** | **Zero, never resold** |

**Landed cost is SAR 124 to 184 per employee per month.** Every comparison in this brain is stated
as landed cost on both sides. Quoting SAR 79 against a competitor seat price hides a third of the
cost, and a competent finance director will catch it in the first meeting.

## The value metric, and why usage pricing is refused

The seat is the primary metric: a knowledge worker with an agent is worth roughly the same to any
employer, so it tracks value without needing to measure it, and it is auditable from a directory.

Usage pricing is rejected on strategic rather than economic grounds. Forecastable cost is one of
only two vectors Wolffish leads, and its credibility rests entirely on taking no margin on
inference. The moment a token earns Wolffish revenue the argument inverts, and a finance director
will spot it. **The absence of a meter is the product.** The seat price does not rise with the quota
tier for the same reason: a customer on the high quota spends more with their own provider and pays
Wolffish the same.

## The deployment scope ladder

**IN BUILD. Proposed, not contracted.** Do not quote these tiers as though they exist.

| Tier | Price | Seats | Integrations | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot | SAR 25,000, credited in full on conversion within ninety days | 10 to 25 | 1 | 3 weeks |
| Standard | SAR 60,000 | 200 to 600 | Up to 3 | 6 to 8 weeks |
| Complex | SAR 95,000 to 150,000 | 600 and above | 4 or more | 10 to 14 weeks |

The pilot is paid, never free. A free pilot spends principal capacity on an unqualified account and
destroys the price anchor in a market where buyers compare notes constantly. The ladder is also a
qualification instrument: an account that resists the complex tier while describing a complex
environment is telling you their budget before you have spent six weeks finding out.

## The capability allowance

**IN BUILD, and the most urgent open commercial item in the brain.** Proposed: two scoped
capabilities per quarter for a standard deployment, three for complex, each under a five-working-day
effort ceiling, with anything beyond quoted as a change order. Unbounded, the arithmetic fails: four
customers each requesting one meaningful integration per quarter at two principal weeks each
consumes a full principal year, leaving nobody to sell or deploy. Bounded, the same four consume
about sixteen principal weeks, which is sustainable.

**Until it is contracted, the phrase "no new invoice" must not appear in writing.** Use "scoped and delivered inside the quarter".

## Negotiation posture

Hold the seat price. A volume discount rewards a larger customer for something that costs Wolffish
nothing extra, and it converts "the price is the price", a genuine differentiator against quote-only
competitors, into "the price is negotiable", which is what everyone else offers.

Concede on term length, on deployment scope inclusions and on quota tier support. Saudi enterprise
negotiation expects movement and refusing all of it reads as inflexible. Quarterly in advance is
genuinely favourable where sixty to one hundred and twenty day terms are common, so expect it to be
challenged and defend it: it is working capital for a two-person company. Trade term length for
payment timing, never the reverse.

## The three return anchors

**Breakeven.** At 300 seats the landed cost is SAR 464,400 a year against a knowledge-worker value
base of roughly SAR 60 million, so it repays itself at a productivity gain of **0.77 percent**.
Replace the modelled SAR 200,000 fully loaded cost per worker with the customer's own figure during
the exposure audit, which roughly doubles the force of the argument and costs nothing.

**Avoided exposure.** One enforcement decision under the personal data law reaches SAR 5 million.
Deliver it only after the architecture walkthrough: first it is fear selling, after it is arithmetic.

**Alternatives.** Say plainly and first that Microsoft's assistant is cheaper per seat, and that the
modelled do-it-yourself gap is small. Then argue governance rather than price.
