# Customer journey

Distils the customer journey funnel. Load this to place an asset at the right stage and to
forecast honestly against the Saudi calendar.

Seven stages per audience. The security call moves from its usual position at stage three to
stage two, which is the highest-return process change in this go-to-market.

## ICP 1, founder-led

| Stage | Weeks | What happens | The asset that carries it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem awareness | 2 to 8 | They learn their exposure is unmeasured and their forecast is missing | Scorecard, cost calculator, founder writing, events |
| 2. Security call | 2 to 4 | Thirty minutes, founder-led, **with the security team in the room** | The call itself, plus the architecture diagram sent beforehand |
| 3. Vendor evaluation | 4 to 10 | Economics, integration feasibility, and a pack procurement can use | Free one-week exposure audit, Compliance Evidence Pack, live residency session |
| 4. Pilot cohort and decision | 6 to 12 | Ten seats, three weeks, one named workflow, in their perimeter | Pilot sprint at SAR 25,000, credited on conversion within ninety days |
| 5. Value realisation | 6 to 8 build, then 12 | Scoping, build, go-live, first audit answer produced | Weekly demonstrations, quota workshop, enablement per function |
| 6. Expansion | Months 4 to 12 | A second department asks for access | Quarterly capability review, seat expansion pricing agreed at deployment |
| 7. Renewal | Month 10 to 12 | Timed **before** the December budget statement, not after | Annual landed-cost review against the original forecast |

Total cycle, first contact to signature: sixteen to thirty-four weeks, plus six to eight to
go-live.

## ICP 2, channel-assisted

Same seven stages, roughly double the duration, with three differences that matter.

Enterprise architecture clears the deal before procurement ever sees it, so stage two carries more
load and needs an architecture document showing that Wolffish adds no new external dependency.
Stage three adds portfolio entry, which must happen before the budget cycle closes or the deal is
deferred two quarters regardless of merit. Stage four is scoped to one business unit at twenty-five
to fifty seats rather than a ten-seat cohort.

## The Saudi calendar

**Calendar weeks are not selling weeks.** This is the single fact most often missed.

| Period | Status |
|---|---|
| September to December 2026 | The live window. About fourteen weeks. Two major events plus the budget cycle. |
| January to early February 2027 | Second window. About five weeks. New budget released. |
| Approximately 8 February to 8 March 2027 | Ramadan. Statutory six-hour working day. Relationship work only. |
| The days following | The first religious holiday. Nothing moves. |
| March to May 2027 | Third window, about six weeks, truncated by the second religious holiday. |
| June to August 2027 | Executive travel. Build and deliver rather than sell. |

**Roughly twenty-five sellable weeks in twelve months, not forty-eight.** A plan that assumes even
months is wrong by about forty percent of the year. A deal that has not reached stage three by
October is a next-year deal and should be managed as one rather than forecast as this year's.

## The four recurring blockers

**"Why not the national champion."** Arrives at stage three in almost every deal. Unanswered it is
fatal. Answered in one sentence with model portability it disappears. Pre-empt it in the security
call rather than defending it in the evaluation.

**Two-person supplier risk.** Surfaces at stage three with procurement and again at stage seven
with internal audit. Pre-packaged escrow converts it into a signed clause. Escrow negotiated
rather than presented becomes the longest pole in the deal.

**The internal build proposal.** Appears after Wolffish leaves the room, proposed by an ambitious
head of digital. Defeated by pricing the maintenance headcount honestly and asking what the
open-source stack produces when a regulator asks for attributed audit and enforced quotas.

**Fiscal-year timing.** The budget statement lands in early December and the fiscal year is the
calendar year.

## The three accelerants

Run stage three **during** the major events rather than around them. Both put the security
officer, the technology lead and often the decision maker in one building for three days, which
compresses six weeks into three days.

Convert stage six into stage one for the next account. A security-officer-to-security-officer
reference call produces better-qualified pipeline than any content. Budget it as a channel.

Build the Compliance Evidence Pack once. It is the largest single time cost in the first deal and
near zero in every one after, which makes the second deal's evaluation materially shorter.
