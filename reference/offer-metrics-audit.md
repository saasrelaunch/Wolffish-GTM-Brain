# Offer metrics audit

Distils the audit of the source Service and Offer Matrix. Load this before quoting anything from
that sheet, and before any conversation about what Wolffish sells. The matrix is self-rated and
split-sourced, and six findings are open. **Nothing derived from it is validated until they are
closed on the review call.**

## Finding 0, two blocks, two companies

Resolved in `positioning-verdict.md`. The founder block governs. The second block is held as a
candidate adjacent offer, flagged, not merged.

## Finding 1, the uncapped commitment

**Ongoing capability development is rated as a free door opener while committing unbounded senior
engineering time against a fixed recurring price.** This is the largest hidden margin risk in the
business model.

The pricing document sells it as an inclusion in the seat, delivered inside the quarter, with no new
invoice. So it is not a free door opener. At 300 seats a customer pays SAR 284,400 a year; one
meaningful integration per quarter at two principal weeks each consumes eight principal weeks, about
the same as an entire new deployment. Across four customers it consumes a full principal year.

**Correction:** embedded in seat, non-billable, capped by a quarterly allowance. See
`pricing-model.md`. Until contracted, "no new invoice" is banned in writing.

## Finding 2, the entry offer is under-rated

The security call is rated as something most competitors can do. True of the activity, false of the
execution: a founder-engineer answering a Saudi security officer's regulatory architecture questions
live in the first meeting, with no pre-sales engineer and no escalation, and saying no if it is not
a fit.

**Correction:** a few others can. This matters because it is the only entry offer in the founder
block, and under-rating it leads directly to under-investing in it.

## Finding 3, escrow lengthens the cycle it exists to shorten

Rated as a long procurement and legal motion, which is honest and describes a trap. Escrow exists to
remove the single-vendor-risk objection. Negotiated rather than presented, it adds a legal cycle to
a deal that would otherwise close faster.

**Correction:** pre-package it. A standing arrangement with a named agent, a pre-drafted deed, and
release conditions already written, presented as a data-room artefact at the security call.
Currently PARTIAL: the deed is drafted and no agent is appointed.

## Finding 4, deployment is a land fee, not margin

Rated a mid ticket. SAR 60,000 buys six to eight weeks of the founder's engineering time end to end,
a below-market principal engagement priced as a land fee. That may be the right strategic choice,
because the seat line is where the money is, but it must be recognised for what it is: **deployment
is a paid pilot that consumes the company's only non-scalable resource, and it sets the hard ceiling
on customer count at four to six a year.** That single number reshapes the entire go-to-market.

## Finding 5, first-deployment effort is understated

Seat delivery is rated templated but customised, the correct steady state and the wrong answer for
deployments one to three, where the template is being written as it is used. **Correction:** heavy
and senior-led for the first three, templated from the fourth. Model it as a margin ramp rather than
a flat assumption.

## Finding 6, which rows from the second block survive

| Row | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Spend diagnostic | **Survives.** Promoted to a co-equal entry offer, extended with the residency dimension, and made the basis of the scorecard. |
| Rollout and procurement support | **Survives as an inclusion, not a product.** Becomes the Compliance Evidence Pack, embedded and free. Charging to clear the buyer's own regulator signals that clearing it is optional. |
| Spend governance platform | Does not survive. Already two capabilities of the control plane. |
| Guardrail design | Does not survive. Deployment work already sold inside the deployment fee. |
| Managed optimisation retainer | **Does not survive, and is hazardous.** A low-price recurring line competing for the same principal hours as an already-uncapped commitment. Recommended: do not sell. |

## The six open actions

1. Confirm or reject the founder framing as the category. Everything hangs on this.
2. **Agree and contract the capability allowance.** Most urgent.
3. Approve the deployment scope ladder and the 200-seat floor.
4. Pre-package escrow and appoint the agent.
5. Decide whether the diagnostic ships founder-led, as a self-assessment, or both.
6. Kill or restaff the optimisation retainer.
