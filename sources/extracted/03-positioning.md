## ⚠️ Standing caveats for this dashboard

**C1 — Not SaaS.** Positioning is written against a **four-layer** product. Statement 1 defends the
harness layer, statements 2 and 3 defend the platform layer, statement 4 defends the service
layer, and statement 5 defends the Saudi layer. A single positioning line covering all four would
be too abstract to survive a meeting.

**C2 — No self-serve.** Every statement below is written to be **said out loud by a founder to a
committee**, not to sit on a landing page above a signup button. The web versions are shorter and
are marked as such.

**C3 — Landed cost, both sides.** Statement 3 makes a cost claim. It is stated as landed cost
including the customer's own inference and infrastructure, and it must never be shortened to
"SAR 79 versus SAR 225" in any medium.

**C4 — Saudi only.** Statements 4 and 5 exist only because of this market. Statement 5, against
the sovereign national champion, has no equivalent anywhere else in the world and is the single
most important thing on this page. The source skill's three-statement template would have
omitted both.

**C5 — Offer metrics unreconciled.** Statement 4's claim that new capabilities arrive "without a
new invoice" depends on the capability allowance in `00-OFFER-METRICS-AUDIT.md` Finding 1 being
agreed and written into the contract. **Until that is done, statement 4 is a promise the company
cannot bound.** Do not put it in a proposal before the allowance exists.

## Step 3.1 — Positioning statements

### Statement 1 — Against direct competitors: the AI vendor clouds
*(Microsoft 365 Copilot · ChatGPT Enterprise · Claude Enterprise)*

> **Not a smarter chat window — an employee who happens to be software.**
> Copilot drafts inside a document and ChatGPT answers inside a tab. Both leave your people as the
> copy-paste layer between an AI and their actual job. Wolffish puts a persistent agent on each
> employee's own machine, with file and command permissions you scope and approval gates you set,
> so the work gets finished rather than started. The difference is not how clever the model is.
> It is what the model is allowed to touch.

*Web version:* Chat drafts. Agents finish. One agent per employee, on their machine, with the
permissions you set.

---

### Statement 2 — Against the residency answer every vendor gives
*(all four vendor clouds)*

> **Not "your data is resident" — your data has nowhere to go.**
> Every vendor on your shortlist will offer you data residency. It is a contractual commitment
> about how they will handle your data after they receive it. Wolffish never receives it: agents
> execute on the employee's device and inference goes to a zero-retention endpoint on your own
> account. There is no path by which a prompt, a file or an output reaches us. That is why you do
> not need an SDAIA transfer mechanism for it, and why the answer survives a question your
> auditor has not thought of yet. Residency you can inspect, not residency you have to trust.

*Web version:* Everyone offers residency. We offer the absence of a data path.

**Delivery note.** This statement is the one that wins the security call, and its power collapses
if it is delivered as a slogan. Deliver it as an architecture diagram the CISO can trace with a
finger, then stop talking.

---

### Statement 3 — Against the metered agent stack
*(Anthropic, and increasingly all of them)*

> **Not a cheaper invoice — an invoice you write instead of receive.**
> On a metered plan the more your agents work, the more you pay, so finance ends up rationing
> exactly the behaviour that creates the value. Uber exhausted its 2026 AI budget by April.
> Seventy-eight percent of IT leaders were surprised by a consumption charge this year. Wolffish
> inverts it: the seat is fixed at SAR 79, the token quota is a hard cap you set per person and
> per team, and inference bills to your own account at a rate twenty to fifty times below
> frontier models. Landed cost is SAR 124 to 184 per employee per month, everything counted,
> against roughly SAR 225 for a ChatGPT Enterprise seat with no agent in it. The saving is real.
> The forecastability is the point.

*Web version:* Fixed seat. Hard quota. Your own inference account. A budget you decide, not a
bill that arrives.

---

### Statement 4 — Against the systems integrator build
*(ejada · Elm · Solutions by stc · SITE — and the true competitor in most Wolffish deals)*

> **Not a project that ends — a product that keeps arriving.**
> Your integrator can build this, and they will build it well, once. Then the second use case is
> a new statement of work, the fourth is a new procurement cycle, and by the third year you are
> maintaining a bespoke platform with people who have moved to another account. Wolffish is a
> product you deploy, not a project you commission: the deployment is fixed at SAR 60,000, the
> platform is SAR 79 per employee per month, and new agent capabilities are scoped and shipped
> inside the quarter without a new invoice. When something better than today's model ships, you
> switch endpoint in a week rather than rebuilding. And if we disappear, source access and escrow
> mean your platform keeps running — which is a shorter answer than the one your integrator gives
> about staff continuity.

*Web version:* An integrator sells you the first use case. We ship the next twelve inside the seat.

**Delivery note.** This is the statement that decides the most deals and it is the one most
likely to be skipped, because the SI is rarely in the room and is rarely named as a competitor by
the buyer. Ask directly: *"if you did this internally or with your integrator, who would own it
in year three?"*

---

### Statement 5 — Against the sovereign national champion
*(HUMAIN — the hardest objection in the Kingdom, and the one a generic GTM has no answer for)*

> **Not instead of the national platform — the layer that puts it on every desk.**
> HUMAIN is building the Kingdom's compute, its Arabic frontier model and its agent marketplace,
> and choosing it is a decision nobody is criticised for. We are not an alternative to that and we
> are not trying to be. Wolffish is model-portable by design: if you want ALLaM on an in-Kingdom
> endpoint, we run on it. What we add is the part a national platform is not built to do for one
> company — a persistent agent on each of your employees' machines, wired into your own
> undocumented admin tool, with your quotas, your approval gates and your audit log. Sovereign
> infrastructure and a deployed workforce are different products. You will most likely buy both.

*Web version:* Sovereign models are infrastructure. We are the layer that puts them on every desk,
inside your perimeter.

**Delivery note — the most important tactical instruction in this suite.** Never position against
HUMAIN. HUMAIN scores 9.8 to Wolffish's 6.8 on sovereign legitimacy (Dashboard 02) and the gap
cannot be closed, only made irrelevant. The instinct to say "we are more sovereign than the
sovereign champion" is both false and, in this market, faintly insulting. Answer the question
before it is asked, in the security call, in one sentence, and move on.

---

### Statement 6 — Against the status quo
*(doing nothing, shadow AI, and the internal DIY build)*

> **Not "you have no AI" — you have AI you cannot see, cannot cap and cannot audit.**
> The choice in front of you is not whether your people use AI. Eighty-one percent of Saudi
> enterprises already deploy it, and the ones who have not sanctioned it have it anyway, on
> personal accounts, with company data in the prompt and no transfer basis on file. The realistic
> alternatives are three: keep the shadow estate and hope nobody audits it; assemble an
> open-source stack internally, which works until the first NCA or SAMA review asks for
> attributed action-level audit and enforced quotas; or deploy something with the controls
> already in it. All three cost money. Only one of them produces the artefacts a regulator asks
> for. Let us show you what your exposure looks like this month, free, and you can decide after
> that.

*Web version:* Your people already use AI. The only question is whether you can see it, cap it
and audit it.

## Message hierarchy for the Kingdom

## The "we are not" list

Saying these plainly, early and unprompted is what makes the rest believable. In a market where
every vendor overclaims, the disqualifier is the credibility instrument.

- **We are not the best model.** We run open weights chosen for cost per token. If your use case
  needs frontier reasoning on every call, we are the wrong architecture, and we will say so on the
  security call.
- **We are not an ecosystem.** We will never have Microsoft's integration breadth and we are not
  competing for it.
- **We are not sovereign infrastructure.** HUMAIN is. We run on top of whatever endpoint you
  choose, theirs included.
- **We are not a large company.** Two people. That is why source access and escrow are in the
  contract rather than in the objection-handling script.
- **We are not a mobile-first product.** The agent's value comes from executing on a machine with
  files and a shell. A phone app would be a worse product pretending to be a broader one.
- **We are not able to take every customer.** Deployment consumes principal time. Four to six in
  the first year. If the fit is wrong we will tell you on the first call rather than in month
  four.
