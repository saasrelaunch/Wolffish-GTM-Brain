# Dashboard 05 — Buying Committee

**Wolffish Cloud · Kingdom of Saudi Arabia · v1.0 · 4 September 2026**
*Source skill Phase 5, steps 5.1 and 5.2, with four Saudi-specific personas added per ICP and a design-partner profile.*

---

## ⚠️ Standing caveats for this dashboard

**C1 — Not SaaS.** A four-layer product has a four-part committee, and they are not the same
people. Layer A is sold to the user and their manager. Layer B is sold to the CISO and the CFO.
Layer C is sold to the CIO and the business owner of the internal system. Layer D is sold to
procurement, legal and compliance — who in most GTM plans are treated as obstacles and who here
are **the audience for the strongest argument in the product**.

**C2 — No self-serve.** There is no path where a user adopts and the committee ratifies later.
Every persona below must be satisfied before anyone signs, which is why the security call comes
first and the demo comes second.

**C3 — Landed cost.** The CFO persona is the only one who will independently check the arithmetic.
Give them the three-line breakdown unprompted. A CFO who discovers the pass-through line
themselves will treat everything else in the pitch as similarly incomplete.

**C4 — Saudi only.** The source skill's three-persona model (User, Manager, Decision Maker) is
**structurally insufficient for Saudi enterprise**, where the CISO can veto unilaterally,
procurement and legal apply a documented process that a foreign-invoiced vendor fails, and a
relationship sponsor with no formal role can move a deal further in one call than the entire
committee moves in a month. Four personas are added below and the skill's original three are
retained unchanged so the deviation is visible.

**C5 — Offer metrics unreconciled.** The "assets to get buy-in" rows assume the corrected offer
stack, in particular the Compliance Evidence Pack as an embedded free deliverable and escrow as
pre-packaged rather than negotiated.

## Committee choreography — the Saudi sequence

Order matters more here than in most markets, and getting it wrong is usually unrecoverable.

| Step | Who | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Relationship sponsor** | Warm introduction only. Cold entry to a Saudi enterprise costs roughly two quarters and usually fails. |
| 1 | **CISO, with the decision maker present if possible** | The security call. Wolffish's strongest argument goes first, to the person with the veto. Winning here converts the gatekeeper into the internal champion, which is the whole strategy. |
| 2 | **CIO / Head of Digital** | The integration conversation and the "could we build this ourselves" conversation, held deliberately rather than avoided. |
| 3 | **CFO** | The landed-cost conversation, with the pass-through line volunteered before it is found. |
| 4 | **Manager and pilot cohort** | Scope one real workflow. Three weeks. Ten seats. |
| 5 | **Decision maker** | Presented with a pilot result, a CISO who is already convinced and a CFO who has the number. Not asked to evaluate a vendor. |
| 6 | **Procurement and legal** | Handed a complete pack rather than a request. |

**The anti-pattern this replaces:** the standard Western sequence starts with the economic buyer
and treats security as a late-stage compliance hurdle. In Saudi Arabia that sequence produces a
deal that dies in month four when the CISO sees it for the first time and asks a question nobody
prepared for.

## Step 5.2 — Design partner profile

The first two or three customers are not customers. They are the reference base, the deployment
template and the proof that the escrow clause has never needed to be exercised.

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Design partner attributes** | Saudi-owned, 250 to 500 knowledge workers, regulated or IP-sensitive, with a CISO who has already blocked a public AI tool and a CEO or owner who can approve without a tender. Has run one AI pilot that stalled — this is the single strongest predictor, because they have already spent the enthusiasm and now want the boring parts. Has at least one internal system nobody will integrate with. Willing to be named as a reference. Located in Riyadh or the Eastern Province so the founder can be physically present within a day. |
| **Job titles** | Primary: CEO / Managing Director / Owner. Co-primary: CISO or Head of Information Security. Sponsor: CIO or Head of Digital. Cohort owner: a department head in claims, underwriting, credit, legal or engineering. |
| **Demographics** | Saudi nationals in the senior roles, 35 to 55, often with Western postgraduate education and fluent English but preferring Arabic for internal work. Present at LEAP and GAIN. Active on LinkedIn in English and on X in Arabic. Reachable on WhatsApp once introduced. Decisions made in person; confirmations sent by message. |
| **Responsibility areas** | Enterprise outcome (CEO) · regulatory exposure and third-party risk (CISO) · systems and integration (CIO) · one function's throughput (cohort owner) |
| **Current tools** | Microsoft 365, frequently with unused or under-adopted Copilot licences · an ERP, often SAP or Oracle · at least one bespoke internal application built years ago by a departed team · a blocked or grudgingly tolerated public AI tool · WhatsApp as a genuine business system of record |
| **Key pain points** | An uninventoried shadow AI estate with no transfer basis · a stalled pilot · an internal system every integration project has refused to touch · knowledge work that scales only by hiring under Saudization economics · a CISO who can neither approve nor stop what is happening · a CFO who cannot forecast the line |
| **Critical triggers** | The CISO has just blocked a tool · a surprising AI invoice has arrived · a pilot has stalled visibly · a new CDO, CAIO or CISO has been appointed · a SAMA, NCA or internal audit finding names AI or third-party data · attendance at LEAP, GAIN or Black Hat MEA · the Q4 budget cycle |
| **Goals and motivations** | To sanction AI rather than police it · to be the company in their sector that got past the pilot · to raise output per head without headcount · to hold evidence a regulator will accept · to be first without being reckless |
| **Decision criteria** | Where does the data go, provably · what can the agent touch and who approves it · what does it cost per employee, all in, and will that number hold · what happens if you disappear · can you reach our internal system · will our Arabic-speaking staff actually use it · how long until something works |
| **Influence level** | Very high. In a market this relationship-dense, a named reference in the same sub-industry is worth more than any marketing asset Wolffish can produce, and a bad first deployment is close to unrecoverable in a network where everyone's CISOs know each other. |
| **Barriers to purchase** | Two-person supplier concentration risk · no SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certificate yet · no reference deployment to inspect · shell access on employee devices sounds alarming before approval gates are explained · deployment fee is capex-shaped · escrow adds legal review · fiscal-year timing |
| **Information sources** | Peer CISOs and peer CEOs, first and decisively · Black Hat MEA and GAIN · SDAIA and NCA guidance · LinkedIn · Arab News, Argaam and Saudi Gazette business coverage · sector WhatsApp groups · the Big 4 and their local partners |
| **Success metrics** | Time saved on one named workflow, measured before and after · percentage of the cohort still using the agent daily at week six · landed cost per employee per month against forecast · number of internal systems reachable by an agent · audit queries answerable from their own store within one day · shadow AI usage reduction |
| **Early adopter characteristics** | Has been burned by an AI pilot and wants the governance parts rather than the demo parts · comfortable being named publicly · willing to give the founder direct access to their CISO and their internal systems · treats the supplier as a partner rather than a vendor, which in Saudi business culture is a genuine and common posture rather than a slogan · patient through a six-to-eight-week build because they have watched an eighteen-month SI project |

### What Wolffish gives a design partner, and what it must ask for

| Wolffish gives | Wolffish must ask for |
|---|---|
| Deployment at or below the standard SAR 60,000, with scope agreed generously | A named public reference, with logo and a quotable sentence |
| Direct founder access throughout, no account manager | Introduction to two peer organisations in their network |
| Their integration priorities shape the roadmap for two quarters | Permission to publish an anonymised deployment write-up |
| Price protection on the seat for the first 24 months | A CISO willing to speak to another CISO on a call |
| Pre-packaged escrow at no charge | Honest, documented feedback at weeks 3, 6 and 12 |

**Do not give:** free seats, an indefinite pilot, or unbounded capability development. The first
two destroy the price anchor for every subsequent deal in a market where buyers talk to each
other constantly, and the third is the Finding 1 exposure with no ceiling at all.
